## Executive summary

- **Growth is strong and volume-driven.** Monthly net revenue rose from $62.2K in Jan 2024 to $226.6K in May 2026. Comparing like-for-like January–May periods, 2026 net revenue is 65.4% above 2025 and profit is 66.7% above. AOV moved only from $128.59 to $126.64, while purchasing customers rose from 2,611 to 4,166. Growth came from more buyers, not bigger baskets.
- **Product-level profit does not cover marketing.** Over 2024-01-01 to 2026-05-31, product-level profit was $1.42M at a 39.0% margin, while recorded marketing spend was $3.97M. The paid channels spend $1,199–$1,670 per new customer, against first-year profit of about $143 per customer. The gap is narrowing: spend stayed roughly flat while revenue grew, so marketing fell from 169% of net revenue in 2024 to 68% in 2026 (Jan–May).
- **Discounting erodes margin without a measurable refund benefit.** Margin falls from 40.8% on full-price orders to 22.2% at discounts of 21% or more.
- **Support load is growing faster than orders.** Tickets per 100 fulfilled orders rose from 11.7 to 24.3, and CSAT drops sharply when resolution takes longer than 48 hours.
- **Churn risk can be ranked.** On the held-out test set, a logistic-regression model reaches ROC-AUC 0.785 and PR-AUC 0.613, against a 26.7% churn base rate. Contacting the riskiest 20% of customers would reach 47.8% of churners at 63.7% precision. These figures come from a synthetic label.

## Recommendations (prioritised)

Priority reflects the size of the effect in the data and how directly the data supports the action. Each recommendation is a **hypothesis to test**, ideally with a controlled experiment, before scaling.

1. **Re-examine paid acquisition budgets (highest priority).** Paid Search and Paid Social take 59.9% of spend, with spend per new customer equal to about 11× first-year profit per customer. Run geo or holdout tests that cut paid spend to measure incremental customers before reallocating budget towards lower-cost channels (Organic Search, Email, Referral: $338–$637 per new customer). *Monitor:* incremental new customers, cost per incremental customer, first-year profit per customer, payback period.
2. **Test a tighter discount policy, carefully.** Margin at 21%+ discounts is 22.2% against 40.8% at full price, and discounted orders show no significant difference in refund rate (p = 0.15). Discount users are also the most valuable customers: the Promotion-Driven Frequent Buyers segment produced 57.2% of 12-month profit with the lowest churn rate (16.7%). Cutting discounts across the board could therefore lose more in frequency than it gains in margin. Run a randomised test of smaller or capped discounts (or loyalty rewards instead of percentage discounts) before changing policy. *Monitor:* margin %, order frequency, revenue per customer and churn in test against control.
3. **Protect support quality as volume grows.** Add capacity or triage so that low-priority tickets do not exceed 48 hours, and investigate why tickets per order are rising (for example, delivery delays and damaged items). *Monitor:* tickets per 100 orders, share of tickets over 48 hours, CSAT and satisfied %.
4. **Use the churn score for capacity-limited retention outreach.** Rank customers by risk score and contact the top segment that fits team capacity; for example, the top 20% reaches about 47.8% of churners. Measure impact with a randomised holdout group, because a score only predicts risk and does not show that outreach works. *Monitor:* retention uplift against the holdout, cost per retained customer, and model calibration over time.
5. **Treat segments differently** (details in `docs/methodology.md` and Notebook 03): reward and cross-sell the high-value segments, run win-back tests for lapsed customers, and fix root causes for refund-prone buyers (product quality, sizing, descriptions). *Monitor:* segment migration month over month.

## Limitations and data needed to validate

- **Synthetic data and label.** Patterns come from a simulation, and model performance shows how well the simulator's logic is recovered, not real predictive accuracy.
- **No cost data beyond merchandise cost.** Shipping, fulfilment, payment, support and overhead costs are needed for true contribution margin and customer lifetime value.
- **Attribution.** Channel results rely on a single recorded `acquisition_channel`. Multi-touch or incrementality data (holdout tests) is needed to judge marketing ROI.
- **Ticket-to-order linkage.** Support tickets have no `order_id`, so refunds cannot be traced to products or orders through support data.
- **Single snapshot.** The churn model was trained on one snapshot date. Production use would need several snapshots (time-based validation), drift monitoring and recalibration.
- **Left-censored history.** Customers who signed up in 2023 have no observed orders before 2024.
